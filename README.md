Bit-depth and Banding: Reducing the bit-depth (e.g., from 8-bit to 4-bit) drastically reduces the number of available intensity levels. This is directly related to visible banding or posterization, where smooth intensity transitions (like skies or faces) are replaced by abrupt steps between the few available quantized levels.Contrast Stretching: Contrast stretching flattens the peaks and spreads out the values in the histogram (from the original range, e.g., $0.2-0.8$, to the full range, $0-1$). This improves the visibility of details in the mid-tones by utilizing the full dynamic range of the display.Aliasing (Nyquist): Aggressive downsampling causes aliasing because the sampling rate (the distance between pixels in the smaller image) becomes too low to capture the highest spatial frequencies (fine details/edges) in the original image. The Nyquist theorem states the sampling frequency must be at least twice the highest frequency present in the signal; when this is violated, the high frequencies "fold over" and appear as false, low-frequency patterns in the sampled signal (aliasing).
